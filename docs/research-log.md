## Process audit — same day

The modular relaxation results in the preceding section are **invalidated**. The first implementation bounded the edge-potential difference to `[-(q-1), q-1]` before taking its residue. That is mathematically wrong because the unnormalized difference can be much larger. The code has been corrected to apply modulo reduction directly to the full linear expression. Modular rows must be regenerated before they are used as evidence.

Separately, too much time was spent attempting to construct Nauty graph input through `nauty-amtog`. That exploration did not alter any mathematical result, but it delayed canonicalization work and was not surfaced quickly enough. Canonicalization remains outstanding; NetworkX VF2 is currently available only for pairwise isomorphism checks.

Valid results unaffected by this audit:

1. all positive-control classifications;
2. rank-potential UNSAT classifications for all five negative benchmarks;
3. fixed-span INFEASIBLE results across every legal span for those benchmarks;
4. saved graph JSON and labelled hashes.

Immediate corrections to complete:

- rerun and overwrite modular reports using the corrected relaxation;
- test both a valid coloring and a deliberately corrupted coloring through the verifier;
- replace or wrap Nauty canonicalization with a tested graph6 pipeline.

## 2026-08-22 — Lane 2 partial-hub scan

Generated every connected simple graph obtained by:

1. choosing a connected core `H` with minimum degree at least 2;
2. subdividing each edge once;
3. adding one hub adjacent to at least four subdivision vertices;

## 2026-08-22 — Lane 6, first synchronization attempt

Replaced the seed's single degree-11 hub by two hubs `U0,U1`, partitioning the eleven connector adjacencies between them. Added one new subdivision vertex `W` adjacent to both hubs as the only direct synchronizer. This preserves the connector-core incidences and gives final order 21.

Searched all masks with both hub degrees between 4 and 9 (final maximum degree at most 9), then relaxed the cap to maximum degree 10:

| final degree cap | unique graphs | colorable | non-colorable | timeout |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 9 | 36 | 36 | 0 | 0 |
| 10 | 42 | 42 | 0 | 0 |

Reports are `results/lane6-split-hub.json` and `results/lane6-split-hub-delta10.json`. The simple two-way split with a single common subdivision edge does not preserve the effective high-degree span; it is too weak as a synchronization gadget. A useful next version needs a terminal gadget that forces the two hub palettes to concatenate rather than merely overlap at one color.

A second synchronization experiment replaced the common subdivision edge by one, two, or three internally disjoint length-4 paths `U0-S0-T-S1-U1`. All masks with both hub degrees at least 4 and final maximum degree at most 10 were tested.

There were 73 non-isomorphic candidates. All were colorable and no solve timed out. Report: `results/lane6-chained-sync.json`.

The combined Lane 6 evidence indicates that splitting the seed hub destroys the obstruction locally rather than merely failing to synchronize the two halves exactly. Future gadget work should first compute finite terminal signatures for small colorable synchronizers, then search for a composition that forces palette concatenation; blindly adding longer paths is unlikely to help.
